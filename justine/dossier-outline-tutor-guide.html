Screen by screen: what a good answer sounds like

Screen 4

Take the question apart

Ask first

What is each part of the question about? Which of your documents are about pressure, and which are about freedom?

Calibration

“The self we show to others” is the image we give. “Family and society expect something else” is pressure. “To what extent can we shape” is how much freedom we have. A good sorting: Eveline, “Boys Don’t Cry” and the start of Brooklyn are about pressure. The later part of Brooklyn and Rockwell are about choice. Brooklyn can go in both parts, because it changes over time. That is a good sign: it shows she understands the book. Rockwell can also go in both: he constructs an image (choice), but we never see his real face straight on (limit).

Screen 5

Choose a plan

Ask first

Which plan would you pick, and why?

Calibration

Plan B (two parts) is the strongest for “to what extent”, because the question asks for both limits and freedom. Plan A is acceptable and safe: say so if she chooses it, and show her it can be improved by adding a link and a nuanced closing. Plan C works if she is confident. Her reason matters more than her choice. The button adds plan B titles to the outline; if she picks another plan, type her part titles into the Part I and Part II rows by clicking them.

Screens 6–10

The documents

For each document the question is the same: say the four moves. Listen for these things, in this order of importance:

  1. Does she answer the question, not only describe? The most common weakness is a good summary with no link to the question. If she summarizes, ask: “How does that answer your question?”
  2. Does she use one precise detail? If not: “What exactly do we see or read?”
  3. Does she link to another document? If not, ask: “What does this remind you of in your dossier?”

Screens 12–14

Opening and closing

Ask first

What detail would you start with? Then: what is your answer to your question, in two or three sentences?

Calibration

Opening. A good hook is one precise detail from a document, not a general statement about identity. If she starts with a general sentence, accept it and ask for a detail. The opening must contain the theme, the question, the four documents, and the two parts.

Closing. The question starts with “to what extent”, so “yes” or “no” alone is not enough. A good closing says how far (“to a large extent”, “partly”), says what limits it (“but…”), and uses at least two documents. If she says only “we can shape ourselves”, ask: “Is that true for Eveline?”

Screen 16

Possible questions

Let her answer each aloud before you click. The answers on screen are models. “Is there a document that does not fit well?” is a good one to push on: honest weaknesses are a strength in an oral, as long as she can explain why she still chose the document. The personal-document question is not on the screen; ask it and listen. Do not supply her reason.
