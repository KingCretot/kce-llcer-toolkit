Brooklyn — Your Notes

The beginning, up to right before Eilis leaves for America

These notes stop exactly where your reading stops — nothing here happens after this point in the book. Use it to check your understanding, not to skip ahead.

Characters

Eilis LaceyThe main character. Young, quiet, and pays close attention to things without saying much about it. She doesn't have a lot of opportunities where she lives.
Rose LaceyEilis's older sister. Confident, has a good job, more comfortable talking to people than Eilis is. She's the one who sets the plan to America in motion — and, it turns out, she's quietly sacrificing her own chance to leave by doing it: staying behind with their mother likely means Rose won't marry either.
Mrs. LaceyTheir mother — a widow. She depends on her daughters, and the idea of Eilis leaving is hard for her, even if she doesn't say so directly.
Father FloodAn Irish priest working in Brooklyn. He helps arrange the job and the place for Eilis to live in America, through Rose.

The Story So Far

1

Eilis lives a small, limited life in her Irish town. There isn't much work for her there, and she's often in Rose's shadow.

2

Rose and Father Flood arrange for Eilis to go to America — a real job and a real opportunity, things she can't easily get at home.

3

Father Flood lays out his case to Eilis directly — a real job waiting, a place to live, someone to turn to if she needs help. It's presented as simple and generous. Whether it actually is that simple is worth questioning.

4

Eilis doesn't fight the plan, but she doesn't really choose it either. She goes along with it.

5

In the weeks before she leaves, her brothers agree to pay for her passage, and Rose handles the paperwork. The night before she goes, Rose helps her pack and gives her jewelry and money.

6

During that night, Eilis realizes Rose is sacrificing her own future to make this happen for her — and says nothing about it. That's where your reading stops.

Themes to Think About

Choosing vs. Being Chosen For

Nobody forces Eilis to go. But nobody really asks her either. She just... doesn't say no.

Is going along with something the same as choosing it?
Duty to Family

Rose is doing this because she loves Eilis and wants better for her. Eilis, in turn, doesn't want to disappoint Rose or her mother by refusing.

Can loving someone be a reason you stop listening to what you actually want?
Feeling Sad Before Anything Has Even Happened

Eilis hasn't left yet — but there's already a heaviness in how she's living these last days at home.

Can you grieve something before you've even lost it?
Persuasion and Motive

Father Flood's offer sounds generous. But he's not a neutral party making it — he's also the one making the case.

Can an offer still be genuine if the person making it also gains something when you accept it?
Silence and Sacrifice

Rose is giving something up too, quietly. So is Eilis, by not saying what she actually feels. Nobody in this scene says out loud what they're really going through.

Is staying silent to protect someone else its own kind of sacrifice — or just another way of not choosing?

The Conflict Right Now

This isn't resolved yet — and that's the point. Eilis is caught between two things: the safe, familiar, limited life she knows, and an opportunity she didn't fully ask for. She hasn't found peace with the decision. She's just... not resisting it.

There's a second layer to it now, too: Father Flood's offer is doing a lot of the convincing here, and he's not a neutral party making it. That doesn't make him wrong — it just means the offer and the person making it are both worth weighing.

And a third: Rose is paying a real, quiet price for this too — and Eilis knows it, and says nothing. Whether that silence is protecting Rose or just avoiding something harder is still completely open.

Your reading ends before she leaves, so this tension is still completely open. Don't feel like you're missing something — nobody knows how she really feels about it yet, including Eilis.
