The dossier

Four documents. They are not four separate things — they are four voices on the same question, and part of your job is to work out what that question is. We meet them one at a time, over several weeks. There's no rush to compare them until you actually understand each one on its own.

Quatre documents. Ce ne sont pas quatre choses séparées — ce sont quatre voix sur une même question, et ton travail c'est aussi de trouver laquelle. On les rencontre un par un, sur plusieurs semaines. Pas de précipitation à les comparer avant de vraiment comprendre chacun.

ABrooklynLiterary · 2009

Read from your own copy: Part Two — the passage where the letters from home reach Eilis in Brooklyn, and what happens to her afterward. Roughly three pages. Read it twice: once for what happens, once for what she does not say.

How we meet this one: vocabulary first, then read together, then you retell it in your own words. No analysis yet — just make sure you really understand it.

BThe decade that emptiedHistorical · non-literary

Ireland in the 1950s lost people the way a slow leak loses water. Work was scarce, wages were low, and for a great many young people the choice was not between staying and leaving but between leaving for England and leaving for America.

Whole villages thinned. Families kept a bed made up for someone who would come back for two weeks each summer and then go again. The word people used was not emigration. It was "going away," as though it were temporary, and for some it was.

What the records show is departures. What they cannot show is the arrangement families made with themselves: that a daughter's leaving was a success, and that nobody would say otherwise out loud.

How we meet this one: before you read a word, you'll guess what "The decade that emptied" is about from the title alone. Then we read it and see how close you were.

CA departure photographIconographic

Look at the image together. A quayside farewell — Irish emigrants boarding, 1950s. Look at who faces the camera and who faces away, where the hands are, what people are wearing, and who is standing slightly apart from the group.

An image is a document, not decoration. It argues something. Your job is to say what.

Une image est un document, pas une décoration. Elle affirme quelque chose. À toi de dire quoi.

How we meet this one: a cold read — you see the photo with zero context first, describe what you notice and guess what's happening, and only after that do we say what it actually is.

DThe quiet migrationPress · contemporary

Every September the same quiet migration takes place. Across the country, thousands of eighteen-year-olds pack up their rooms and move to cities they have visited twice, at most.

Rural areas lose a large share of their young people within two years of the baccalauréat, and many never return except at Christmas.

What the numbers do not capture is the ambivalence. "People assume we couldn't wait to leave," says Marion, 19, who moved from a village near Limoges to Toulouse last year. "Some of us couldn't. But I still cry when I get off the train home. Both things are true."

How we meet this one: before reading, tell me about a time you felt torn between leaving somewhere and staying. Then we read Marion's quote and see what she has in common with you.

The question

A theme is not enough. Emigration is a theme, and it will get you nowhere. What you need is the tension — the thing these four documents disagree about, or circle without settling.

Our problématique

Three tests. Does it cover all four documents? Does it contain a tension, not just a topic? Could someone disagree with the answer? If any answer is no, it isn't ready.

How the weeks unfold

This runs slower than one month. Comprehension comes before comparison, always — nothing here is timed to a deadline you don't yet have the tools for.

Why the paper disappears in stages

In June you will have no notes at all. Nothing. So we take the paper away in real steps, not just three months on a calendar:

  1. November — write the exposé out in full, and read from it. First time saying it out loud is what matters, not how it sounds.
  2. Nov 16 — the same exposé, but from a mind map. Words and arrows, no sentences.
  3. December — from nothing. Only the documents in front of you. This is what June actually asks of you.
  4. Then we add a document for Séquence 2, and start the ladder again — but faster, because the method is no longer new.

En juin, tu n'auras aucune note. Donc on retire le papier par étapes réelles, pas juste trois mois sur un calendrier. Écrire d'abord, c'est permis — c'est même la méthode.
