1A · The paper

What you are facing

You do not know exactly what the questions will be. So we get ready in three ways: you know the story, you read the question carefully, and you build an answer that is accurate, precise, and explained.

1B · The story

The book in six movements

For each movement, say it aloud in two sentences first. Then check the facts and answer the questions.

1Enniscorthy: a small life and a plan made for her

Eilis lives a limited life in her town, in Rose's shadow. Rose and Father Flood arrange a job and a home in Brooklyn. She goes along without really choosing. On the last night Rose helps her pack.

Who arranges Eilis's move, and does she really choose it?
Rose and Father Flood arrange it. Eilis does not fight the plan, but she does not really choose it either. She goes along.
What does Eilis realize on her last night at home?
Rose is giving up her own future so that Eilis can go, and she says nothing about it.
2The crossing and the arrival

A rough voyage, a storm, and a lot of seasickness. Georgina helps her. Father Flood meets her and settles her into Mrs. Kehoe's boarding house.

Who helps Eilis on the ship?
Georgina, a more experienced traveler who shares her cabin. She helps Eilis through the worst and prepares her for immigration.
Who meets her in Brooklyn, and where does she live?
Father Flood meets her. She lives in Mrs. Kehoe's boarding house with other young Irish women.
3Brooklyn: work, homesickness, belonging

Eilis works at Bartocci's, trained by Miss Fortini and others. She is homesick, and her letters leave out how hard it is. Bartocci's starts to welcome Black customers. She goes to evening classes and passes her first-year exams.

What does Eilis leave out of her letters home?
How hard it is. She is homesick, but her letters do not say so.
What happens at Bartocci's when the new customers come in?
The store has decided to welcome Black customers, framed as a smart business move. Eilis is one of the assistants chosen to serve them. Each time one comes in, the store goes still and watchful, and not one of them ever comes in alone.
4Tony: someone who chooses her

A dance at the parish hall, then dinners and movies. Tony tells Eilis he loves her. At dinner with his family, Frank is blunt. Then Coney Island and a Dodgers game. At Christmas Tony talks about a house on Long Island.

How do Eilis and Tony meet?
At a dance in the parish hall. Tony tells her he is Italian, walks her home, and asks to see her again.
What does Frank say at dinner?
That the family does not like Irish people.
What does Tony hope for around Christmas, and what does Eilis do?
One of the houses his brothers are building on Long Island will be for the two of them. Eilis does not answer directly.
5Rose's death and the secret marriage

Rose dies suddenly. Jack writes that Eilis should come home to look after their mother. Eilis turns to Tony. Before she leaves, they marry at the courthouse and tell no one.

What does Eilis learn about Rose's death?
Rose died suddenly in the night. She had a heart condition that she had hidden from everyone.
What does Eilis do before she goes home for a month?
She marries Tony in a simple courthouse ceremony. They tell nobody.
6Back in Enniscorthy: the choice

Rose's room is untouched. Eilis tells no one about Tony. She stays for Nancy's wedding, meets Jim again, and the town admires her. Miss Kelly shows she knows the secret. Eilis tells her mother and goes back.

Who discovers Eilis's secret, and how?
Miss Kelly, the shopkeeper. She heard about the marriage from Mrs. Kehoe.
What does Eilis do on her last morning in Ireland?
She leaves a note at Jim's door promising to explain, and takes the train. The book ends with her imagining Jim hearing that she has gone back to Brooklyn.
1C · The question

Read the question first

The first word of a question tells you the job. Say the job aloud for each question.

1. “Explain why Eilis keeps her marriage secret.” — what is the job?
Explain / why. Give reasons, and support each with a moment.
2. “Compare Tony and Jim.” — what is the job?
Compare. What they share, where they differ, and what each stands for.
3. “To what extent is Eilis in control of her life?” — what is the job?
To what extent. Not all or nothing: say where she is in control and where she is not, then decide where you stand.
4. “How does Tóibín show that Eilis is homesick?” — what is the job?
How does the author show. Look at his choices: what is said, what is left out, how it is told, and what effect it has.
1D · The answer

Build an answer, step by step

QuestionHow does Tóibín show that Eilis is torn between two homes?
Read the question

What is the job, and what are the key words?

How does the author show is about technique, not only events. The key words are torn and two homes: you need both Enniscorthy and Brooklyn.
Point

Say one sentence that answers the question.

“Tóibín shows that Eilis is torn between two homes by…” · “One way Tóibín shows this is…”
Evidence

Choose one precise moment. Who, where, what happens?

Movements 3 and 6: one moment from each home.
Explain

Say what the moment shows, and why it answers the question. This is the step that earns the points.

“This shows that…” · “This suggests…” · “The effect is that the reader…”
Link

Tie it back to the question, or lead to the next idea.

“So the two places each hold part of her.” · “This is why the ending feels open.”
Your turn: say the whole paragraph aloud, in four or five sentences, using one moment. Say the four steps in order.

2C · The central question

What is a problématique?

It is the question that turns a pile of documents into a dossier. A good one is a question, it is open, it fits the theme, and every document can help answer it in its own way.

Openers: How can…? · To what extent…? · Can we…? · Why is it so hard to…? · What does it take to…?

Warm-up on a different theme (myths and heroes). Say whether each one is weak or strong.

1. “Heroes in stories.” — weak or strong?
Weak. It is a topic, not a question.
2. “Is Superman a hero?” — weak or strong?
Weak. It is a question, but it has one answer and only fits one document.
3. “Do we need heroes to feel brave ourselves?” — weak or strong?
Strong. Open, so different documents can answer in different ways, and it has an angle.
4. “How do stories turn ordinary people into heroes, and what do they leave out?” — weak or strong?
Strong. It has two parts, so documents can agree or disagree, and it can be discussed.
Your turn: choose two or three documents. Say a question aloud that they could all help answer. Then test it: what would each document say in one sentence?
2D · The connections

Three ways to connect your documents

Each lens is a way of looking at all your documents at once. Open one at a time and notice which gives you the most to say.

AShowing and hiding

Showing who you are, or hiding it, or staging it.

  • Jane Eyre: finally says everything she kept inside, and is freed
  • Eveline: keeps everything inside and cannot act
  • Brooklyn: letters that leave things out; the hidden marriage
  • Kahlo: shows pain and identity openly, in a staged setting
  • Sherman: hides herself behind characters
BOther people's expectations

The part played by family, society, and stereotypes.

  • Jane Eyre: an aunt and a headmaster decide what Jane is; she refuses
  • Eveline: duty to family holds her back
  • Brooklyn: Rose, Father Flood, her mother, and Enniscorthy shape Eilis's choices
  • Sherman: the roles movies give to women
  • Ripley, Wonder Woman, “Boys Don't Cry”: stereotypes about who may show emotion
  • Kahlo: builds an identity around her Mexican roots
CStaying and leaving

Becoming yourself, and what you may have to leave behind.

  • Jane Eyre: breaking the “invisible bond”
  • Eveline: staying, and not breaking free
  • Brooklyn: leaving, going back, choosing again
  • Kahlo: painting herself in bed after the accident, to survive
2E · Presenting

A three-minute version to prepare

Introduce the dossier

Name the theme, say your question, say which documents you chose.

First document

What it is, and how it starts to answer your question.

Second document

What it is, and how it gives a different angle.

Third document and your personal document

How it adds to or complicates the others.

Answer your question

Say where you end up, in two or three sentences.

Opening: “My dossier is on the theme of the expression and construction of the self. My central question is: ___. To answer it, I chose ___.”
Linking: “This is relevant to my question because ___.” · “A different angle appears in ___, where ___.”
