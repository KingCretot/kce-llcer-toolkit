Session Blueprint

One real objective per week. A story with visible wins, not a checklist.

Scope: Oral-First

This program's primary objective is the oral half of the exam — the presentation and unscripted exchange, worth 50% of the grade. That's what a live 60-minute Zoom lesson can genuinely train, week after week.
The written half gets real but light-touch support: translation is drilled every week as spoken practice, and the tableau comparatif / problématique work — which also feeds the written synthèse method — happens conversationally, out loud, on the shared screen. There is no timed written exam simulation anywhere in this plan.

The Weekly Frame — Open / Core / Close

Not a checklist to march through. One real focus per week — never more than one new cognitive demand — because she arrives mid-afternoon, straight from a school day. The shape below respects that, and always ends in speech.
Open · 5–10
Pick Something. No prep, no pressure, no stakes — she's talking within the first sixty seconds of every single session, before anything hard begins.
Core · 35–45
This week's one real job — see the objective in the table below. Comprehension, or comparison, or dossier work, or rehearsal. Never all of them at once.
Close · 10–15
Always ends in speech — the exposé, a mock exchange, or a toolkit game. She leaves every session having said something, not just read or listened.
Default
Every week's Toolkit Rotation below states its Open and Close explicitly. Where Open isn't named, it defaults to Pick Something on the current deck. Where Close isn't named by a specific tool, it defaults to an unaided one-minute retell of whatever Core covered that day.
Woven in
From Phase 2 onward, Say It in French takes a fixed 5-minute slice inside Core, every week — spoken translation, her fastest points, never skipped.
