Your Dossier Oral — Study Notes

Building an outline for your oral presentation on expression et construction de soi

Use these notes before and after the lesson. They show you how to turn your four documents and your central question into an outline you can speak from. Your documents may be different at the real Bac, so the method matters more than the examples. For the facts about each document, see also your earlier notes, section 2, and the Triple Self-Portrait notes.

1. What the oral asks

You will deliver an oral presentation on your chosen theme, expression et construction de soi, and connect each of your documents to that theme. Your teacher told you your works are very good. Your real Bac oral will follow the same format, probably with different works, so this is practice for the method.

What you do not know yet: how long the presentation is, whether you get preparation time, and how many documents each row of the cover page needs. Ask your teacher. Until then, prepare a short version of about three minutes and be ready for questions. Speak from key words, not from a written text.

2. Your outline at a glance

This is the outline you built with the four documents on your cover page. Your teacher may ask for a different order. The shape is what matters.

Why two parts? Your question starts with “to what extent”. That asks for a nuanced answer: how much, and what limits it. Part I shows the limits (pressure). Part II shows the freedom. Together they answer the question. A plan document by document is safer, but it can sound like a list. A plan with three lenses (showing and hiding, expectations, staying and leaving) is richer but harder to keep clear in a short talk.

3. Your four documents

Four moves for each: what it is · what it shows · how it answers my question · link to another document. This is the same shape as a Brooklyn answer: Point, Evidence, Explain, Link.

“Eveline” (Dubliners)
James Joyce · short story · 1914 · literary text · Part I

What it is: Eveline, a young woman in Dublin, lives with her father and works in a shop. A young sailor, Frank, has promised her a new life in Buenos Aires.

What it shows: Her mother is dead, and she has taken on many family responsibilities. She has to choose between the fear of the known and an uncertain future abroad. Your class noticed the contrast between past and present, freedom against duty, and the use of “used to”.

How it answers your question: She cannot say what she wants, so she cannot build a different life. Duty and fear shape the self she shows, and she stays.

Link: Brooklyn. The situation is almost the same (leaving home and family for a new life) and the outcome is the opposite.

“Boys Don’t Cry”
The Cure · song · 1979 · non-literary text · Part I

What it is: A song in which a man hides his sadness by laughing, because boys are expected not to cry.

What it shows: Society’s expectations about who you should be affect what you allow yourself to express.

How it answers your question: Society decides which feelings he may show, so the self he shows to others is a mask.

Link: “Eveline”. In both, expectations stop someone from showing or acting on who they are: duty in one, a rule about feelings in the other.

Brooklyn
Colm Tóibín · novel · 2009 · full work · Part I (start) and Part II (later)

What it is: A novel about Eilis, a young Irish woman who emigrates to Brooklyn, builds a life there, and has to choose between two homes.

What it shows (start): Her move is arranged by her sister Rose and by Father Flood. She does not truly choose it. She often says little about how she feels, and her letters leave things out.

What it shows (later): Eilis marries Tony in secret at the courthouse before she goes home. When Miss Kelly finds out, Eilis tells her mother, leaves a note for Jim, and goes back to Brooklyn. Her control over her life grows through the book.

How it answers your question: At first, others shape her life. Later she controls what each person knows, until Miss Kelly forces the secret out. Then she acts. Her control grows through the book, but it takes pressure to make her act.

Link: “Eveline” (Part I). Rockwell (Part II): both choose what to show to others.

Triple Self-Portrait
Norman Rockwell · painting · painted 1959, on the cover of the Saturday Evening Post in February 1960 · personal document · Part II

What it is: An oil painting made as a magazine cover. We see the painter from behind, a mirror, and the portrait on his canvas.

What it shows: In the mirror, glare on his glasses hides his eyes. On the canvas, he has no glasses and his eyes are clear. At the upper right he pins small self-portraits by Dürer, Rembrandt, Picasso, and Van Gogh.

How it answers your question: He constructs the image he shows. But there are three selves in one picture (the man from behind, the reflection, the portrait), and we never see his real face straight on. So he has choice, and also limits: which one is real?

Link: Brooklyn: both choose what others see.

Your notes: See the Triple Self-Portrait notes for what to look at and the words to describe it.

4. Linking your documents

A link is the sentence that turns four documents into one dossier. Choose two or three strong links. Do not try to link everything.

LinkWhat you can say
“Eveline” and BrooklynSame situation: a young woman can leave home for a new life. Opposite outcome: Eveline stays, while Eilis leaves, goes back, and chooses again.
Brooklyn and RockwellBoth choose what others see. Eilis chooses what each person knows. Rockwell constructs the image he shows, but we never see his real face straight on.
“Eveline” and “Boys Don’t Cry”Both show expectations that stop someone from showing or acting on who they are: duty in one, a rule about feelings in the other.

Sentence frames

  • “Both ___ and ___ show that ___, but in different ways.”
  • “In ___, ___, whereas in ___, ___.”
  • “If you read ___ next to ___, you can see that ___.”
  • “___ answers my question by showing ___, while ___ complicates it by ___.”

5. Opening and closing

The opening: write it last

  1. Hook. One detail that makes people want to listen. Example: in the mirror, Rockwell’s glasses hide his eyes, but on the canvas his eyes are clear.
  2. Theme and question. Name the theme. Say your question.
  3. Your documents. Name all four.
  4. Roadmap. Tell your listener the two parts: “First… then…”
A possible opening: “My dossier is on the theme of the expression and construction of the self. My central question is: to what extent can we shape the self we show to others when family and society expect something else? To answer it, I chose Brooklyn, “Eveline”, Rockwell’s Triple Self-Portrait and “Boys Don’t Cry”. First, I will show how family and society push us to hide or to stay. Then I will show that we can still shape what we show.”

The closing: answer the question

Your question starts with “to what extent”, so the answer cannot be only yes or no. Say how far, and what limits it.

A possible closing: “To answer my question, I would say that to a large extent we can shape the self we show: Eilis takes control step by step, and Rockwell constructs the image he shows. But we are never completely free: duty keeps Eveline at home, a man is told not to cry, and even in Rockwell’s painting we never see his real face straight on. Even when we choose what to show, the real self may stay hidden.”

These are models. Your own words are better. For more openers, see section 5 of your earlier notes.

6. Questions you may be asked

Think about each one in advance and have a short answer with an example.

Where do two of your documents disagree?“Eveline” and Brooklyn: both face family pressure, but Eveline stays and Eilis leaves, goes back, and chooses again.
What does Brooklyn add to your question?It shows change over time. Eilis moves from being directed by others to taking control step by step, so it answers “to what extent” with a process, not a yes or no.
Is there a document that does not fit well?“Boys Don’t Cry” is about a man and his feelings, so it fits Part I better than Part II. Say it honestly, and say why you still chose it: for example, it shows clearly how society decides what we may express.
Why did you choose this question?All four documents show someone deciding what to show to others: what they hide, what they stage, and what they choose.
Why did you choose your personal document?This one is yours. Use your Rockwell notes, and say what you noticed that made you want to talk about it.
Which document is the most important for your question?There is no one right answer. Choose one, and give two reasons.

7. The method for any works

At the real Bac your works may be different. These five steps still work.

  1. One sentence per document. What is each one really about?
  2. Find what they share. Turn it into a question: “To what extent…?”, “How can…?”, “Why is it so hard to…?”
  3. Sort your documents. Which show pressure or limits? Which show freedom or choice? That gives you two parts.
  4. Four moves for each document. What it is · what it shows · how it answers the question · link.
  5. Opening last, closing answers the question.

8. Words and common mistakes

Words for a nuanced answer

How farto a large extent · to some extent · partly · up to a point · never completely
Adding a limitbut · however · although · even though · yet
Comparingwhereas · while · unlike · in contrast · on the one hand… on the other hand
Explainingthis shows that · this suggests that · this is why · as a result
Time in a storyat first · later · in the end · from then on
The themeto hide · to show · to stage · to express · to shape · to expect · pressure · duty · a mask

Watch out

eventuallymeans “in the end”, not “maybe”. “Eventually, Eilis goes back.” For “maybe”, say “perhaps” or “possibly”.
pretendmeans “to act as if something is true when it is not”. It is not the French “prétendre”. For “to claim”, say “to claim” or “to say”.
peoplenot “persons”. “People expect…” In general statements, say “society”, not “the society”.
in the end / at the end“In the end” means “finally”. “At the end of the book” means the last pages.
make a choicenot “do a choice”. “Eilis makes her own choice.”
on the other handnot “in the other hand”.
